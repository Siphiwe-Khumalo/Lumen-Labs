# Creative Direction — client amendment (AUTHORITATIVE)

This note is an explicit client amendment to the cinematic-film brief, captured
after the client reviewed the plan. The design phase (CINEMATIC-FILM.md) and the
design reviewer MUST treat this as a hard requirement. It sharpens emphasis; it
does not relax any existing constraint (brand system, no-AI-visuals, licensing,
isolation, verification all still apply).

## The one-line brief

**Make a short film, not an ad.** The client's words: "a storyline not an ad...
i want it wow, like it's a movie lowkey, something i will post over and over
again." Optimize every decision for *rewatchability* and *emotional impact*, not
for information delivery.

## What this means concretely (bake into the storyboard)

1. **A real protagonist and a real story.** There is ONE business owner we follow
   — a specific human, shown through real licensed footage (hands, posture, a
   face half-lit at a desk at night, the quiet weight of running a business
   alone). We feel their frustration before any brand or service is mentioned.
   Give the character a visible arc: tension -> a turning point -> relief and
   momentum. The viewer should care about the person by ~15 seconds in.

2. **Open on a scene, not a statement.** No headline card to start. Start inside a
   moment: late light, a cluttered desk, a missed call, a system that won't talk
   to another system, an empty client pipeline. Earn the first words of narration.

3. **Cinematic grammar.** Think opening of a film, not a product reel:
   - Let shots BREATHE. Hold on quiet frames. Use silence and negative space as
     tools. Do NOT fill every second or move every shot.
   - Vary shot rhythm: long contemplative holds in Act 1, a clear accelerant at
     the turn, confident measured beats in the resolution.
   - Depth, layered compositions, real light and shadow, consistent film-grade
     color so footage + motion graphics read as ONE world.
   - Transitions are motivated (a match cut on light turning warm, a reveal that
     mirrors the character's realization) — never template wipes/spins.

4. **The turn is the money moment.** When Lumen Labs enters, the shift must be felt
   in the body: music lifts, the grade warms, the frame opens up, the character's
   posture changes. This is the trailer beat people replay. Do not undersell it,
   do not make it cheesy. Lumen Labs is the calm, capable partner who shows up —
   not a magic tech montage.

5. **Services as story, never a list.** Software, web apps, IT infrastructure,
   networking, cloud/M365, cybersecurity, CCTV, VoIP, IoT, digital systems appear
   ONLY as visible relief in the character's world (screens now connected, the
   room now calm and monitored, the pipeline now flowing). If it reads like a
   capability checklist, it has failed. Weave them into the transformation.

6. **Narration = inner voice / storyteller, sparse.** Calm, youthful, human.
   Fewer words than feels natural — let images carry meaning. Lines land in the
   gaps between visual beats, not over every frame. Leave long stretches with
   music and picture only.

7. **On-screen text is rare and earned.** Minimal, large, lots of air, long holds.
   The ending message sequence ("BUILT WITH INTENTION." / "Your business has a
   vision. We build what brings it to life." / "Small enough to care. Technical
   enough to build." / "LUMEN LABS") is the emotional landing — give each line
   real room, do not cram.

8. **Ending like a film's final frame.** The logo lockup should feel like the last
   shot of a movie: settled, confident, a frame you'd screenshot. Hold it.

## Footage selection guidance (for the acquisition step)

Favor emotionally legible, cinematic, human clips over generic "tech b-roll":
- Act 1 (struggle): a person alone at a desk at night, tired/stressed body
  language, rain on a window, an empty office, a phone that doesn't ring, papers,
  a cluttered or dim workspace, cold blue light.
- The turn: first warm light (sunrise through a window, a lamp warming), a second
  person arriving/collaborating, a calm conversation, a hand on a shoulder energy.
- Act 3 (transformation): warm golden-hour workspace, screens showing real
  dashboards/code, server/network rooms shot beautifully (not sterile), people
  working with purpose, a city/business coming alive, growth and motion.
Pick clips that CUT TOGETHER as one story with consistent light direction and
grade-ability. Quality and emotional fit beat quantity.

## Non-negotiables unchanged

Brand tokens verbatim (graphite grounds, single restrained amber accent that may
warm as the story resolves, Space Grotesk / Manrope / JetBrains Mono); no AI
people/offices/synthetic environments; no neon/holograms/glass/particles;
licensed keyless stock only (Mixkit video + edge-tts VO + royalty-free music);
existing 27s film untouched; 1920x1080; 75–90s; typecheck green; render a real
MP4 with embedded audio + VERIFICATION.md.

If any instruction here appears to conflict with the original brief, prefer the
STORY/CINEMATIC emphasis — that is the client's explicit priority.
